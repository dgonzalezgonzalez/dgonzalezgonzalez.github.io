OECD consultancy

Demand-side policies for sustainable food choices

Consulting work from April 2025 to April 2026 for the OECD Trade and Agriculture Directorate's Agro-food Trade and Markets division on demand-side policies for sustainable food choices. The work combines policy research, empirical analysis, and applied data tools to study how governments can encourage more environmentally sustainable food consumption across OECD member and accession countries.

My contribution has included policy stocktaking, literature review, classification of policy instruments, and comparative analysis of approaches such as consumer education, voluntary collaboration, regulation, fiscal measures, public procurement, food-based dietary guidelines, and public canteens. The work also examines stakeholder engagement, household acceptability, evidence gaps, and trade-offs with affordability and public health.

On the technical side, I developed retrieval-augmented generation workflows and semantic analysis tools to structure policy information, compute cosine-similarity policy-alignment measures, extract evidence on policy effectiveness and evaluation results, and compare policy texts across strategic dimensions. I also used text embeddings and k-means clustering for semantic policy classification, and carried out econometric analysis to estimate the effect of Denmark's livestock emissions tax announcement on beef consumer prices, using difference-in-differences, synthetic control, and synthetic difference-in-differences methods.

Supporting Food Systems Policy Workstreams: Analytical, Digital and Capacity Development

Current consulting work (since June 2026) for the OECD Food Systems Team on analytical, digital, and capacity-building workstreams related to food systems policy. One part of the assignment focuses on a RAG-based meta-evaluation of food loss and waste policies, classifying evaluated policies and synthesising evidence on their effectiveness.

I am also developing an interactive online resource for the OECD Toolkit for Equal Opportunities in Food Systems. The tool will present existing analysis in an accessible format for policy-makers and interested users, with a dashboard, self-assessment features, and a specialised RAG-based chatbot built on the relevant documentation.

A third part of the project focuses on capacity building: preparing materials and teaching a practical course on AI applications to policy analysis for the OECD Food Systems Team. The course covers embeddings, retrieval-augmented generation, agentic coding workflows, MCP, skills, Git basics, and hands-on examples using policy data.

Fun GitHub projects

  • ape-policy-meta-analysis - reproducible meta-analysis of Project APE's automated policy evaluations. It screens 699 paper families to obtain 626 comparable standardized estimates, uses cached Jev annotations to classify outcome direction, and analyzes binary-treatment and continuous-exposure estimates separately. The package includes random-effects synthesis, sensitivity analysis, frozen inputs, and offline numerical replication. See the draft paper.
  • danish-meat-tax - analysis of beef prices, slaughter, and trade during Denmark's 2024 cattle-policy transition. It combines official Danish and European data with difference-in-differences and synthetic DiD estimation, exploratory grocery price-change diagnostics, and conditional carbon-damage scenarios. Concurrent nitrate regulation limits attribution to the tax announcement, and the European price comparison is imprecise. See the draft paper.
  • faolex-analysis - analysis of more than 40,000 FAOLEX food legislation records, including demand-side and supply-side classification, abstract-based embeddings, translation caching, cosine-similarity analysis against food-policy strategy queries, time trends, country maps, and a unified interactive dashboard.
  • canarias-cantabria-unis-ml - Python pipeline for Canary Islands and Cantabria job postings and Spanish university degree catalogs. It scrapes public employment and job-board sources, normalizes geography and contract fields, builds a degree catalog, gates candidate job-degree pairs, computes text-embedding alignment scores, and stores outputs in CSV and SQLite.
  • prestaciones-por-empleo - Python pipeline for SEPE monthly unemployment-benefit reports. It downloads new or modified Excel files, builds a Spanish-language dataset, and produces static figures and an interactive dashboard. The dashboard covers contributory benefits and unemployment subsidies, with demographic and territorial filters, eligibility causes, subsidy breakdowns, a territorial map, and CSV downloads.
  • Spain-AI-Exposure - replication package for research on AI exposure and registered unemployment across 502 Spanish occupations, January 2021-March 2026. It maps Anthropic/O*NET exposure to CNO-2011 occupations, replays frozen embeddings and Jev measures, and runs the empirical analysis in Python, Stata, and R. The current manuscript documents a gradual unemployment divergence by exposure; aggregate new contracts show no persistent response. See the IEB working paper and updated manuscript.
  • replication-tracker-jpe-ej-ectj - local-first tracker for JPE, JPE Macroeconomics, JPE Microeconomics, The Economic Journal, and The Econometrics Journal. It pulls paper metadata from OpenAlex, maps papers to Dataverse and Zenodo replication repositories, reads repository files and documentation, classifies data availability, and exports dashboard JSON.
